- **jq: a `try` or `(...)?` around a `reduce` `UPDATE`'s navigation of the accumulator is now read as navigating, so after a source that moved the register the path error it raises is caught as jq catches it** (#4459).
  `(reduce .[]? as $i (.; try (.a | not) catch 1)) // .a` on `{"a":true}` printed `["a"]` where jq raises on the handler's `1`, and `del(...)` of
  it deleted `.a`; `isempty(try .a)`, `try isempty(.[]?) catch 1` and `(., try isempty(.a) catch 1)` were wrong the same way. The other direction
  too: `try .a`, `try .[]?`, `(.a)?` and `try .a catch false` as the `UPDATE` were refused where jq falls through to the `//` alternative and
  answers `["a"]`, and `try .a catch "x"` now quotes the caught `"x"` as jq does. The fold kept the accumulator at the register and ran the body
  by value, so the error never happened; the body now goes through the live resolver, which catches it, and the handler (which runs only after a
  raise) is not read. Among a comma's siblings a caught item is skipped only when a later sibling certainly yields (`reduce` keeps the last
  output, so `((.k)?, .)` stays `[]`), and never inside `first(...)`. Over the 94 `try`/`?`/`isempty` fold operands in
  `scripts/jq-path-register-sweep.py` (18 added): wrong accepts 50 -> 10 on the 83,499 `--stage-only` rows, and 33 -> 16 and 32 -> 20 on two
  50,000-row seeded samples of the full grid. The cost is lost answers in the safe direction, 132 stage-only rows and 13 and 9 in the samples:
  almost all a caught item **last** in a comma after `.` (`(., (.a)?)`, `(., try .a)`), which jq answers `[]` and is now refused because
  skipping it is what let `(., (isempty(.a))?)` write, plus a fold with a caught `UPDATE` as the left of an `and`/`or` inside a `//`.
