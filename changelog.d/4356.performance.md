- **jq: `path(.[$i:][0])` no longer builds the slice per read** (#4356). A computed slice of the pipe's own input followed by an integer-literal index (`path(E[a:b][k])`, and through it `=`, `|=` and `del`) built the slice in the resolver and then wrote it out and re-indexed it through the bridge to step into it, so a loop over reads at a moving bound was quadratic. It is now answered from the slice's resolved range at both places, naming the slice's component as the bounds were spelled and then the index as written (`path(.[1:][-1])` ends in `-1`; an index past either end of the slice is `null`). Over 4,000 elements, `[range(length-1;-1;-1) as $i | path(.[$i:][0])] | length` takes 9.7 ms (was 1,995 ms) on an Apple M4 Pro and 12.3 ms (was 2,237 ms) on a Ryzen 9 7950X; over 2,000 elements 6.5 ms (was 503 ms) and 6.8 ms (was 538 ms). The old times quadrupled per doubling; the new ones grow 1.5x and 1.8x, as the `path(.[$i])` floor does (1.5x and 1.8x on the same two machines), and the floor and the already-fused value-read control are unchanged (release builds, interleaved min of 5, one core on the 7950X). jq mode, a trackable array and bounds that never read `.`; a float-spelled index, a generator bound and yq keep the general route, recorded in `limitations.md`.
