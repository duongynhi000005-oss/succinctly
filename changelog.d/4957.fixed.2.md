- **jq: a caught-bind `foreach` as the UPDATE of a `foreach` over a source that leaves the register on the root answers as jq does** (#4957). `[path(. as $x | foreach (1,2) as $i (.; if . then (foreach (try ($x as {a:$a} | .)) as $y (.; .; .)) else . end; .a))?]` on `{"a":null}` is `[]` in jq and exited 5 here with `Invalid path expression with result {"a":null}`, a refusal no `?` or `try` catches. The accumulator is the register at those steps, so `$x` bound at the root is it: the pattern's step passes, the EXTRACT's `.a` raises jq's own index error, and the `?` catches it. The same holds for the sources `.`, `..`, `.,.a`, a `catch .` handler, a caught bind of `.` itself, and the `try`, comma, pipe and `if` spellings of the UPDATE, in `path`, `del`, `|=` and `=`. Only a `foreach` whose source is one bind of a one-variable pattern whose body is `.` is read (its INIT, UPDATE and EXTRACT may be anything); a `?//` chain, a nested or multi-variable pattern, a body other than `.` and a `select` stage keep the refusal they had (reading them flipped rows to an answer jq refuses or to a caught error where jq answers). Over 211,680 rows of six differential grids against `/usr/bin/jq` 1.7.1 and a `main` build: 5,803 rows that were refused or answered differently now match, none that matched differ but one that changed an error's wording while staying refused, and none is newly accepted where jq refuses.
