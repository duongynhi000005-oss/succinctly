- **yq: `del()` vivifies a `null` along multi-valued steps and computed keys, as yq's growing traversal does** (#4922, #2126).
  `del()`'s pre-pass followed only plain field, position and literal-bracket steps, so `{"a":[null,{"b":null}]} | del(.a[] | .["b"]["c"])` (yq `{"a":[{},{"b":{}}]}`), `{"x":[null,null]} | del(.x[]["a"])` (`[{},{}]`), `{"a":null,"n":"x"} | del(.a[.n])` (`{"a":{},...}`) and `{"a":null} | del(.a[(0,1)]["k"])` (`{"a":[{},{}]}`) left the `null`s alone. The walk now fans out over `.[]` (arrays, objects, and a `null`, which becomes `[]`), `..`, a comma ahead of a bracket and a multi-key bracket, evaluates a computed key against the whole document, and pads a short array out to the position a later bracket vivifies (`del(.a[5]["k"])` on `[1,null]`). Over a 39-row table of those shapes the binary went from 20 matches to 36; a 2,800-target random differential against yq v4.53.3 (small documents, targets of one to four steps from a pool of fields, positions, brackets, `.[]`, `..` and commas) gained 13 to 33 matches per 700 and lost 0 to 2, all of the kind recorded in `limitations.md` (sibling comma branches that disagree on a `null`'s kind). The read-side twin (`.y = (.x | del(.["k"]))`) is unchanged.
