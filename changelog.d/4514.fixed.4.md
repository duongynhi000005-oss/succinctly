- **jq: a `foreach` source whose first output is the register and whose later outputs are not (`f((., 1))` for a `$` parameter, `limit(2; ., 1)`) now raises where jq raises** (#4514).
  `path(foreach (limit(2; ., 1) as {a:$a} | .) as $k (.; .; .))` on `{}` printed `[]` and `del(try foreach (limit(2; ., 1) as {a:$a} | $a) as $k (.; .; .))` on `{"a":true}` printed `null` (the document deleted)
  where jq exits 5 (`Invalid path expression with result {..}`). jq runs the first output first, and its destructure moves the register before the second output exists; the by-value drive ran every output and answered
  the later one's error, which the `try` swallowed. The register spine walk now also asks whether the *first* output of a bound expression is the register (a comma's first leaf through nested commas, a `limit` with a positive literal count, `nth(0; ..)`, `try`/`?`/`first`/`label` and a pipe with `.` over one, a frozen `$x`), for a bind source, a `$` parameter's argument and the variable a bind introduces. A `(., 1) as $b | $b as {a:$a}` was accepted the same way and is fixed with it.
  Contexts where the operand's `.` is not the register (a negated operand) refuse rows jq answers, as `first(., 1)` and `limit(1; ., 1)` already do.
  Still open on #4514: a call with no arguments whose body is such a source (`def f: (., 1); f as {a:$a}`, which a routed `def f: ., {a:1}` pins as a refusal of a negated operand jq answers) and a `reduce (label $o | .)` source followed by a destructure (a refusal, jq answers).
