- **jq: a `foreach` UPDATE `if` whose literal `then` ran beside a fold in the `else` answers on the last step of every INIT fork, not only the last fork** (#5113). jq stores the literal as the state, and the next INIT fork replaces it with INIT's own output, so the literal feeds no later UPDATE: `[path(. as $x | foreach .a as $i ((.,.); try (if .a then 1 else FE end) catch 1; $i))?]` on `{"a":{"a":null}}` (with `FE` a `foreach` over `try ($x as {a:$a} | .)`) is jq's `[["a"]]` where it answered `[]`. A step that is not its fork's last (`foreach (.a,.)`) still resolves the `if` whole, as the next step of the fork would run the fold on the stored literal. The same holds over an INIT whose count is unbounded (`(.,.) | .`), as only the source's bound says the step is its fork's last. A `?//` pattern, or an UPDATE holding a `?//` chain, keeps reading it on the `foreach`'s last step only: a retry runs UPDATE again on the stored literal, and a later fork whose fold source is a chain was answered as empty. Over 20,000 rows of `scripts/jq-foreach-update-if-arm-sweep.py` and 25,000 more with `null`, `true` and `false` literal arms added, against `/usr/bin/jq` 1.7.1 and a `main` build, rows that were refused or answered differently now match and no row moved to a worse answer.
