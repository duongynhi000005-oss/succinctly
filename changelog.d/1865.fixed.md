- **yq: `del()` over a comma group with a literal target follows yq's back-to-front loop** (#1865). yq walks the targets
  of `del()` in reverse and stops at the first one with no parent, keeping what it already deleted, so `del(.a, 1)` deletes
  nothing while `del(1, .a)` deletes `.a`, and `del(.a, .c, 1)` deletes nothing. succinctly raised `Invalid path expression`
  for every such group, and for a lone literal (`del(1)`). A literal, constructor or root now ends the scan the way it does
  in yq, in objects, arrays and fan-outs alike (`del(1, .a[])` empties `.a`; `del(., 1)` keeps the document, `del(1, .)`
  empties it). A target that is a derived value or a bound variable (`del(.b | tostring)`, `del(.a, (.b as $y | $y))`)
  still refuses, and jq mode is unchanged.
