- **jq: a comma beside a bare caught-bind `foreach` UPDATE is read item by item, so the `foreach`'s emission is judged against the register the bind moved and a literal sibling's against the register the step entered UPDATE with** (#5018). `try path(. as $x | foreach (.a,.) as $i (.; (foreach (try ($x as {a:$a} | .)) as $y (.; .; .) | .), 7; null)) catch "C"` on `{"a":null}` is `["a"]` `["a"]` `"C"` in jq and answered `"C"` alone here, the `catch` handler's output where jq answers two paths (`[path(..)?]` dropped them the same way). jq runs each comma item from the register UPDATE was entered with, and only the `foreach`'s bind moves it onto the bound child, so the `foreach` item's `null`/boolean EXTRACT is that child's node and the `7` item's is the entry register's node or jq's own raise. The comma was read as one UPDATE whose register the `foreach` had moved, so it was refused as a guess. The comma of one such `foreach` (alone, piped into stages that cannot move the register, or behind `try`/`?`) and literal siblings (`7`, `null`, `true`, `false`, `"s"`, `0`), in either order and with `empty` items around them, is now resolved one item at a time, each item with the flags it would have as an UPDATE of its own. A sibling that is not a literal (`.`, `$x`, `.a`, `[]`) keeps the answer it had.
