- **jq: a `reduce` nested in a `reduce` UPDATE whose source or loop pattern is a `?//` chain is no longer refused where jq answers** (#4506).
  `del(reduce 1 as $k (.; reduce (. as {a:$a} ?// {b:$b} | .) as $k (.; .)))` on `{"a":false,"b":null}` is `null` in jq and exited 5 here
  (`Invalid path expression with result {...}`), and so did the `path()`, `=` and `|=` twins and `. as [$a] ?// {b:$b}` or
  `... | $a` as the chain. The vetter that decides the nested fold hands its accumulator through (`foreach_source_is_path_checked`) excluded
  every `?//` chain in the source for fear that a failed alternative leaves `null`; the resolver retries the alternatives as jq does,
  and a chain whose every alternative fails raises in both (`. as [$a] ?// [$b]` over an object is `Cannot index object with number`).
  A chain as the nested fold's own *loop pattern* (`reduce (. as {a:$a} | .) as {a:$q} ?// $q (.; .)`, `null` in jq) answers too when its
  last alternative is a bare `$var` and its UPDATE is a no-op: such a chain cannot raise whichever alternative binds, and jq restores the
  register on a `reduce`'s exit, so the first alternative's pattern step, which the walk can only guess at after a source that moved the
  register, is retried instead of refused (`reduce_alternative_is_immaterial`). A chain whose last alternative can fail still refuses.
  Over the 15 loop-pattern operands added to `scripts/jq-path-register-sweep.py` (16,407 `--stage-only` rows) against `main`: 268 refusals become matches,
  no match is lost and there are no wrong accepts.
