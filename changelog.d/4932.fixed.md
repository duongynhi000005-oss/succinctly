- **jq: a `reduce` UPDATE that destructures an outer `$x` is no longer refused where jq answers when the binds are wrapped in a comma, `label`, `first`/`limit`/`nth(0; ..)` or `if`, or end in `select(true)`, `(.)?` or `try . catch .`** (#4932, #4901).
  `path(. as $x | reduce 1 as $i (.; if . then ($x as {a:$a} | .) else . end))` and `path(.a as $x | reduce .a as $i (.; label $f | $x as {a:$a} | .))` on `{"a":{"b":1}}` are `[]` in jq 1.7.1 (their `del()` deletes the document, `|= 9` replaces it); they refused with `Invalid path expression with result <accumulator>`. A `reduce` stores its UPDATE's output without a path check, so a chain of single-pattern destructures of `$x` that hands `.` on unchanged hands the accumulator back whatever the wrappers around it are; the wrappers are read through only where every output of the body is that `.` and nothing is caught (a `try`/`?` around a bind would turn a raising bind step into an empty UPDATE, which is data-dependent and stays refused). A 23,040-row sweep against `/usr/bin/jq` (three outer binds x eight sources x three INITs x forty UPDATE shapes x `path`/`del` x four documents), run on a 5,760-row hash sample, moved 197 rows from a wrong refusal or wrong wording to a match, none stopped matching and none newly accepts where jq refuses; an `if` whose condition navigates (`if .a then ..`) stays refused. Still refused or worded differently (recorded in `limitations.md`): a bind behind a `try`/`?` or a `?//` chain, a computed key, a `. // 1` or `select(.)` tail, a pipe headed by `$x`.
