- **jq: a `foreach` UPDATE `if`/`elif` chain with the caught `foreach` in a later arm is read as that arm on the steps that run it** (#4800).
  `[path(. as $x | foreach . as $i (.; if .a then 7 elif . then foreach (try ($x as {a:$a} | .)) as $y (.; .; .) else .a end; $i))?]` on `{"a":null}` is `[]` in jq and was refused naming the state, uncatchably under `?`, because only the outermost `if`'s `then` was read for the step that took the `elif`.
  The chain is now walked as the `else` side already was: a falsy condition continues into the `else`, and the first truthy one names its `then`, so `elif` spellings of `if . then FE else .a end` answer as jq does under `path`, `del`, `|=`, `try ... catch` and `?`.
  The read applies only where the chain is read as empty on the step that runs the `foreach` with `$x` off the register (the mirrored `if C then B else (if . then FE else E end)` is now read in a `foreach` UPDATE too): an `elif` condition other than `.` and a fold in two arms keep the refusal they had, since marking the next step there turned it into a wrong accept in the grid.
  Against `/usr/bin/jq` 1.7.1, three random 12,000-row samples of a 144,000-row grid (450 `if`/`elif` UPDATEs x 4 sources x 4 EXTRACTs x 4 write contexts x 5 documents) fix 114, 96 and 92 rows, lose none and accept nothing wrongly.
