- **jq: a `?//` chain that destructures `.` in a `reduce` UPDATE over a source whose first element is the accumulator itself is no longer accepted where jq raises** (#4969, #4964 residual).
  `echo '{"a":1}' | jq -c 'path(reduce .. as $i (.; try (. as {a:$a} ?// $a | $a)))'` raises `Invalid path expression with result 1`; it answered `[]` here, `del(...)` deleted the document and `(...) |= 9` wrote through it (`{"a":{"b":1}}` and `(.,.a?)`, `first(..)` sources likewise). `..` yields `.` first, so at step 1 the accumulator (INIT `.`) is jq's register and the first alternative's pattern step passes; the binary marked the step as one whose source moved the register off the accumulator (true for `.[]?`, whose elements are children), refused alternative one as exact and retried onto the bare `$a`, all of `.`, keeping the root through every later step. The move marks are now skipped for the step where the element is the register's own node and the accumulator is already at it, for an UPDATE holding a `?//` chain with a bare `$name` alternative (every other shape, including a chain of two patterns, keeps its wording). Pinned by `tests/jq_cli/i4969.rs`. A 10,530-row grid against `/usr/bin/jq` moved 66 wrong accepts and 33 wrong refusals to a match and lost none. Still different (recorded in `limitations.md`): INIT `.a` over `first(..)` is still accepted, some chains ending in `.` over other spellings of the register are refused where jq answers, and the wording names the accumulator rather than jq's `null`.
