- **jq: a `catch` handler around the `foreach` a `foreach` UPDATE is made of emits where jq's does** (#5004). `[path(. as $x | foreach (.a,.) as $i (.; try (foreach (try ($x as {a:$a} | .)) as $y (.; .; error("x"))) catch (.); $i))?]` on `{"a":null}` is `[[]]` in jq and was `[]` here, and the same filter in `path`, `del` and `|= 9` raised `Invalid path expression with result {"a":null}` where jq answers `[]`, `null` and `9`. jq runs a handler after backtracking to its `try`, which restores the register the step entered its UPDATE with, so the handler's output, and a `$i` EXTRACT after it, sit there; the handler had been run with no register. It is now seeded with that register and its output is read back at it, for a `null` and a container landing, any emitting handler (or a caught `break`), behind a value head such as `null |`, on stdin and with `-n`. A handler inside the nested `foreach`'s own INIT, UPDATE or EXTRACT no longer keeps the refusal of a container landing either. Only for a step that binds one bare `$name`, whose nested `foreach` has one caught destructure as its source and an INIT that cannot move the register: a comma of caught destructures, a two-entry pattern, an outer destructure or `?//` chain, and a navigating INIT keep their refusal (reading them answered or wrote where jq does not).
