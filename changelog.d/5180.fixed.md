- **jq: a destructure whose source navigates two or more steps from a `$x` marker, or iterates it, onto the node jq's register stands on is no longer refused** (#5180). jq's `path_intact` compares the source's `jv` with the register by pointer, and `$x.a.c` is the child handle of `$x`'s node, so it is the register after a stage `.a.c` and the destructure's first step passes. One step from the marker already did (#4827), but a head of two or more steps was evaluated by value, which bridges the second step through a fresh copy, so the pointer test failed and the first step was refused; a `try` swallowed the refusal and `del`/`|=` left the document where jq writes (`del(. as $x | .a.c | try ($x.a.c as {d:$q} | $q))` on `{"a":{"c":{"d":{"b":1}}}}` is `{"a":{"c":{}}}` in jq and left the document here), and `path` raised where jq answers (`path(.a as $x | .a.c.d | $x.c.d as {b:$q} | $q)` is `["a","c","d","b"]`). The head is now also walked straight off the marker's node (names, literal indexes, `[]`), taking each step's child handle as jq does, and the output at the source's position is the register when the two share storage; a head that reaches another node still refuses. Still refused where jq answers (`docs/compliance/jq/limitations.md`): a head behind a pass-through wrapper (`$x | first(.a) as P`), a `?//` chain and a plain `as $q` bind (#5181).
