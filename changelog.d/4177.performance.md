- **jq: the top-level document splitter finds a container's end over a 64-byte mask, not byte by byte** (#4177). After #4172 removed the line counter, `find_matching_close` plus its string scan was the second-largest term of `jq -c .` over a large document: 15-19% of instructions on `wide` and `users` (about 12.7 Ir per input byte on `wide`), 5% on `comprehensive`. `json::light::container_end` now classifies `"`, `\`, control bytes and the container's own brackets once per 64-byte block and answers each hit with a `trailing_zeros`, so the bytes between hits and the per-string scan entry are gone: 3.7 Ir/byte on `wide` 2 MB, 119.0 M total instructions against 137.8 M (-13.7%), `users` -11.6%, `comprehensive` -4.4%, 6.9 MB of 85-byte NDJSON records -6.0%, 2 MB of 8-byte NDJSON records -1.3%. A bracket inside a string is a hit the mask cannot tell from one outside, so the first one in a string swaps the block's mask for one without the brackets until the string ends (a string made only of the container's own brackets was +24% on a 7950X and +60% on an M4 Pro before that). Interleaved A/B wall-clock against `main`, output identical on every row, 21 reps, medians: Ryzen 9 7950X `wide`/`users` -8.9% to -13.6%, `comprehensive` -5.2% to -6.1%, pretty-printed -13.4%, a 5 MB array of 0-256-byte strings -5.3% to -22.6% and of escape-bearing strings -26.5% to -27.9%, arrays of tiny objects/arrays -16.0% and -18.4%, bracket-only strings -2.2% to -11.4%, tiny NDJSON records -2.6% and -2.8%; Apple M4 Pro `wide` -5.2% to -6.1%, `users` -5.1% to -10.1%, `comprehensive` -2.5% to -2.7%, pretty-printed -3.6%, plain strings -4.8% to +0.7%, escape-bearing strings -18.0% to -19.5%, tiny objects/arrays -7.3% and -5.0%, bracket-only strings -5.9% to +1.0%. 20 MB of 8-byte NDJSON records, the shape a per-call block classification cannot help, reads +1.4% and +3.5% on the M4 Pro (same-binary control: -3.3% to -0.6%) and -2.6% and -2.8% on the 7950X.
