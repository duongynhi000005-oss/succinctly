- **jq: a fold whose UPDATE is a pipe of assignments no longer copies the field it extends on every step** (#5285). `reduce .[] as $x ({n: 0, l: []}; .l += [$x.id] | .n += 1)` ran its stages one after another on a clone of the state, so each step copied the array it extended. The stages are now applied to the moved state in turn, a later stage the in-place step declines (a padding or negative index, a failing `+`) runs on the evaluator against the state so far, and the first stage to decline hands the untouched state back as before. Any other pipe, and a stage that could yield other than one value, keep the old route. Measured on an Apple M5 Max (release builds, interleaved, min of 5, ~20 ms process-spawn floor included, output identical to jq 1.7.1): the `reduce` above 0.22 s → 0.022 s at 5,000 records, 0.83 s → 0.028 s at 10,000, 3.15 s → 0.039 s at 20,000 (jq: 0.04 s at 5,000, 0.47 s at 20,000). A pipe whose later stage reads the state into an array operand (`.i += 1 | .a += [.i]`) is 2.4-2.5x faster (`until` at 20,000 steps, 5.38 s → 2.14 s) but still grows with the size of the state, because reading `.i` for the operand does (#5288).
