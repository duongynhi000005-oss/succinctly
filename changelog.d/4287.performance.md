- **jq: a slice reads only its range, so a narrow window of a wide array is no longer O(length)** (#4287). `.[$i:$j]` collected every element of the array before taking its range, so a loop slicing a small window out of a large array was quadratic. A slice now reads only the elements in its range: by index once the array has been read three times (#4035's element index), and otherwise in one walk that counts the array and keeps only the elements a slice with non-negative bounds can hold. Slicing every 8-element window from the back of an array (`[range(length-1;-1;-1) as $i | .[$i:$i+8] | 0] | add`) on a Ryzen 9 7950X: 192 ms to 6 ms at 3,000 elements, 758 ms to 10 ms at 6,000, and 5.35 s to 25 ms at 16,000; on an Apple M4 Pro, 131 ms to 7 ms, 500 ms to 10 ms and 3.5 s to 22 ms. One slice of a million-element array is 10-24% faster on the 7950X and 6-11% on the M4 Pro (`.[$i:$i+2]`, `.[-2:]`), and a slice of almost all of it (`.[:-1]`) is within 2% of before on both. Output is unchanged in `jq` and `yq` mode, including errors: a malformed element inside the range still raises, one outside it still does not, and an array with a malformed delimiter is read as before.
