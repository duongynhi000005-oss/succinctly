- **jq: a destructure of `.` behind a constant-falsy `//` or a catch handler as a `foreach` source, and in the body of a `try ... catch` source, now raises as jq does** (#4677, #4682).
  `path(foreach (false // (. as {b:$b} | .)) as $y (.; .; .))` and `path(foreach (try error catch (. as {b:$b} | .)) as $y (.; .; .))` on `{"a":true}` are `Invalid path expression with result {"a":true}` in jq and were `[]` here, so `del`, `=` and `|=` through them wrote where jq refuses; the same for `null`, `empty`, `false | not | not` and a comma of falsy outputs as the left of the `//`, for `try error({a:1}) catch (...)`, and for the branch as the one live stage of a pipe (`(false // D) | .`).
  `path(foreach (try (. as {a:$a} | .) catch .) as $k (.; .; .))` on `{"a":[1]}` raises the same way (#4682), and on a `null` document answers `["a"]` where it printed `[]`; a pattern that type-errors into the handler (`[true]` against `{a:$a}`) is still answered, as in jq. The register walk read both `//` and `try ... catch` as opaque and sent the source to the by-value drive; #4644 had read them only for a moved `reduce` body. A truthy left, a `try` body that does not raise, a branch that may not run (`limit`, `first`) and a `reduce` source (which backtracks the destructure) are unchanged. Still answered where jq raises: such a source followed by a stage other than `.` or joined by a comma (`(false // D) | select(true)`, `(false // D), 1`), named by a zero-argument `def`, with a non-literal `error(...)` message, or a `try` body that is a `?//` chain of several alternatives.
