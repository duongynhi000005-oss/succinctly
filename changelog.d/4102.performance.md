- **yq: a wide mapping's members no longer cost a scan of the mapping each** (#4102). Two per-position costs scaled with the number of siblings. `BalancedParens::enclose` (a node's `parent`) walked backward one word at a time to the parent's open, so on the k-th member of a mapping it cost k siblings' worth of words; it now skips whole L0/L1/L2 blocks with the min-excess index `find_close` already uses. And every `value()` of a mapping read all its keys to look for a `<<` merge key; a document with no `<<` (or escape that can spell one) in its text now answers that once, from one byte search. Measured on a 128,000-member mapping, `[.[] | document_index]` and `[.[] | head_comment]` go from 3.4 s to 0.18 s and `[.[] | parent | tag]` over 16,000 members from 14.3 s to 0.05 s; `.` on a 10 MB document retires 15-25% fewer instructions (M4 Pro, `users`, `nested`, `comprehensive` and the wide mapping, output identical), and `[.[] | select(.a > 3) | key]` on the wide mapping 55% fewer. `[.[] | keys | length]` on `users` retires 2% more: the one-off byte search. `length`, `keys` and `has` of a mapping still count its members each time, so a fan-out calling them on the parent stays quadratic, as does a computed-key lookup (#4003).
